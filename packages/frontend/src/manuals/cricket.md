# Cricket

A game of claiming numbers rather than counting down. The targets are **20, 19,
18, 17, 16, 15 and the bull**, and nothing else on the board counts for
anything.

## Marks

Every dart on a target scores **marks**: a single is one mark, a double two, a
treble three. Three marks **close** that number for you.

Once you have closed a number and your opponents have not, further hits on it
score points — the number's face value per mark, so a treble 20 on a closed 20
is 60. The bull scores 25 a mark, 50 for the double bull.

When every player has closed a number it is **dead**: it scores nothing for
anybody for the rest of the leg.

## Winning

The leg is won by the player who has closed all seven targets **and** is not
behind on points. Closing everything while still trailing does not win it — you
have to make the points up first, which is why leaving one number open to score
on is the whole game.

## Variants

**Standard** — points go to the player who threw them, and the highest score
wins.

**Cut-throat** — points are pushed onto every opponent who has *not* closed that
number, and the **lowest** score wins. Scoring becomes punishment, and hitting
the leader's open numbers is the way to play it.

## Scored as

Marks per round (MPR), computed over **completed rounds only** — dividing
running marks by a fraction of a round would read a first-dart treble as 9.00
MPR, which means nothing.
