# Gotcha

Counting **up** instead of down. Everyone starts on zero and adds every dart to
their total; the first to land on the target — 201, 301 or 501 — wins the leg.

Three darts a turn, and every ring counts at its face value, so a treble 20 is
60 on the way up just as it is on the way down.

## Overshooting

With **exact target** on, going past the target busts the whole turn: your score
returns to where it stood when the turn began and the rest of your darts go
unthrown. Finishing means landing on the number precisely.

With **reach or pass** on, the target only has to be reached — the first dart to
put you level or above it wins. A gentler game, and a much shorter one.

## The knock-back

This is the mechanic the game is named for. If your dart puts you **exactly on
an opponent's total**, you knock them back:

- **back to zero** — they lose the whole leg's work
- **back to previous turn** — they lose only the turn they last threw

A player sitting on zero cannot be knocked back, and a knock-back that would
change nothing is not applied. There is no limit to it: one dart can knock back
every opponent who happens to share that total, and the leg can turn on a dart
that scored you nothing at all.

The practical consequence is that a big lead is not safe and a round number is
dangerous. Sitting on an opponent's score is an invitation.

## Handicaps

A head start, not a different target. Tick the suggestion box and each player is
proposed a starting score from their recent Gotcha average; the numbers stay
editable and are clamped below the target. A player starting above zero can be
knocked back like anybody else.

## Scored as

Three-dart average, best turn and busts, plus the knock-backs given and taken.
