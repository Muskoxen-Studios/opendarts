# Killer

Everyone gets a number and a stock of lives, and the last player left alive
wins. Nothing is added up — the whole game is about taking lives off other
people.

## Claiming numbers

Before play begins, each player throws for a number of their own. The **first
dart to land on an unclaimed 1–20**, in any ring, claims it for that player. A
player who finds nothing in three darts is handed a random unclaimed number, so
nobody gets stuck in the claiming phase.

Once everybody has a number, play begins.

## Hits

Everything in the play phase is counted in **hits**: a dart is worth its
multiplier on the number it lands in. A single is one hit, a double two, a
triple three. Anything else — a miss, either bull, somebody else's segment — is
worth nothing.

## Becoming a killer

A player is not dangerous until they are a **killer**, and you become one by
landing **three hits on your own number**. Three singles do it, so does a
triple, so does a double and a single. Progress carries over between darts,
turns and rounds — you never start again from nothing.

Once you are a killer, **every hit on an opponent's number** takes **a third of
a life** off them: a single a third, a double two thirds, a triple a whole one.
A player reduced to zero lives is eliminated and skipped for the rest of the
match. Last player standing wins.

## Friendly fire

Off by default. Turn it on and hits on **your own number**, once you are already
a killer, cost *you* a third of a life each. That includes the hits left over
from the very dart that crowned you: two doubles is four hits, three of which
make you a killer and the fourth of which comes straight back at you.

It makes an early kill worth taking carefully, since the board no longer has a
safe number on it for you.

## Lives and handicaps

The starting stock is the same for everyone — three by default, one to nine.
Handicaps here are extra lives: tick the suggestion box and weaker players are
proposed a larger stock from their recent Killer results. The numbers stay
editable, and what the match starts with is what is played.

## Notes on play

- Your own number is both your key and, with friendly fire on, your liability.
- There is no advantage in claiming a "good" number — a 20 is no easier to kill
  with than a 3, and no easier for opponents to hit.
- Because a non-killer's darts are inert, the opening exchanges are usually
  everyone throwing at their own number at once — one turn is normally enough.
- A triple is a whole life, so a killer with a settled triple can end someone in
  three darts.

## Scored as

Lives remaining, kills dealt and lives lost, and the order of elimination.
