# Shanghai

A race through the numbers in order, everyone on the same target at the same
time. Round 1 is the 1, round 2 the 2, and so on up to the last round — 1 to 7
by default, but any window from 1 to 20 works.

## A round

**Only darts on the round's own number score**, and they score their ring value:
in round 3, a single 3 is 3, a double 6 and a treble 9. Everything else on the
board is worth nothing at all.

Everybody throws their three darts at the round's number before the round
advances, so nobody gets ahead of the field. Highest total after the last round
wins.

## The Shanghai

Landing a **single, a double and a treble of the round's number in the same
turn** is a Shanghai, and with **instant win** on it wins the match outright, no
matter what the scores are. The three darts can come in any order.

Turn instant win off and a Shanghai is just a very good turn: 6 × the number,
and the highest total still decides it.

## Why the window matters

Rounds 1–7 is the traditional game and stays winnable for everyone. Starting
higher makes each round worth more and the Shanghai worth chasing; running to 20
makes it a long game where the early rounds barely matter. The chosen window is
the same for every player.

## Scored as

Points per round, the round-by-round card, and hit rate on the round's number.
