# X01

Everyone starts on the same score — 301, 501 or 701 — and throws three darts a
turn. Each dart is subtracted from what is left. The first player to land
exactly on zero, on a dart the out-rule allows, wins the leg.

## A turn

Three darts, then the turn passes. A dart counts its ring value: a single 20 is
20, a double 20 is 40, a treble 20 is 60. The outer bull is 25 and the bull is
50, and the bull counts as a double for double-out purposes.

## Bust

If a turn would take you below zero, land you on 1 with a double-out, or reach
zero on a dart the out-rule does not allow, the turn **busts**: the whole turn
is thrown away and your score goes back to what it was when the turn started.
The remaining darts of that turn are not thrown.

## In and out

| Rule | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Straight | Any dart opens or closes the leg |
| Double | Only a double (or the bull) opens or closes it |
| Master | A double or a treble opens or closes it |

**In** governs the first scoring dart of the leg: under double-in, darts before
the first double score nothing. **Out** governs the finish. Double-out is the
usual match rule, straight-out the usual pub rule.

## Checkout hints

When the score can actually be finished with the darts you have left, the route
appears in your remaining dart slots and is lit up on the board — the dart to
throw now brightest, the rest dimmer behind it. It respects your own out-rule,
so under straight-out 20 is shown as S20 rather than D10.

On a score that cannot be finished — 501, 171, or a bogey number such as 169 —
the slots stay empty rather than offering a finish that is not there.

## Legs and sets

A match runs to a number of legs, and optionally to a number of sets of those
legs. By default a leg ends the moment somebody checks out. Set **leg ends:
when all but one player has checked out** and play continues instead, so
everyone gets a finishing place — the leg is still won by whoever went out
first. With two players the two settings are the same thing.

## Handicaps

A handicap here is a per-player override of the start score and of the in/out
rule, so a stronger player can play 501 double-out against 301 straight-out in
the same leg. Tick the suggestion box and each player's start score is proposed
from their recent three-dart average; the numbers stay editable, and what you
start the match with is what the leg is played off.

## Scored as

Three-dart average, first-nine average, best turn, 180s, checkout percentage
and busts, all per player and all folded from the throws themselves.
